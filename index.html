Deck identity

What we are

Proactive Mardu creature combat. Token generation, attack triggers, extra combats, creature-entry damage and selective protection.

What we are not

Not a dedicated infinite-combo or hard-stax deck. The plan is to win through layered, explosive battlefield synergies.

Table assumptions

Built for multiplayer Commander, usually four players. Pressure multiple opponents without needing to focus every resource on one target.

The supplied export has 100 cards including 36 lands by count. Neriv is treated as the commander, making 100 cards total. This matches the 36-land development baseline.

Mulligan guide

Judge opening hands on mana quality and early action, not just raw land count. This is a practical heuristic, not a validated simulation output.

Usually keep

2–4 lands with workable colors, early ramp or a two-/three-mana play, and a path toward repeated pressure or card flow.

Usually ship

One-land hands without reliable acceleration, awkward single-color hands, or hands loaded with expensive payoffs and no setup.

Context matters

Two lands plus a Talisman and an engine can be stronger than four lands and only late-game spells. Play/draw and opponents' speed matter.

LINE 01 · TABLE-WIDE BURN

Siegebreaker steals the show

Mardu SiegebreakerRed DragonNeriv
  1. With Neriv in play, have Siegebreaker exile your nontoken Red Dragon when Siegebreaker enters.
  2. Attack with Siegebreaker. Its trigger creates one tapped, attacking Red Dragon copy for each opponent.
  3. Each copy enters this turn and its ETB deals 4 to each opponent, doubled by Neriv to 8. Three copies mean 24 damage to each opponent from ETBs.
  4. If each 4/4 flying Dragon copy connects with its assigned opponent, Neriv doubles each hit to 8 more: 32 to each opponent in this ideal scenario.

72 total table damage from the ETBs alone · 96 including all three unblocked Dragon hits

The copies enter attacking; they do not themselves trigger “whenever this creature attacks.” Siegebreaker's own combat damage is not included.
LINE 02 · ONE-SWING KNOCKOUT

Enduring Courage → Neriv → 24 commander damage

Enduring CourageTwinflame TyrantNeriv
  1. Begin with Enduring Courage and Twinflame Tyrant on the battlefield.
  2. Cast Neriv. Courage triggers, granting Neriv +2/+0 and haste until end of turn. Neriv is now 6/5 flying.
  3. Attack an opponent with Neriv. If unblocked, its 6 combat damage is doubled by Neriv (entered this turn) and doubled again by Twinflame Tyrant.
  4. 6 × 2 × 2 = 24 commander damage, clearing the 21-damage threshold in a single swing.

24 commander damage · One opponent eliminated

Neriv must have entered this turn. Twinflame Tyrant doubles damage to opponents, not your own permanents.
LINE 03 · DRAGON AFTERSHOCK

Red Dragon + Twinflame Tyrant + Neriv

Red DragonTwinflame TyrantNeriv
  1. Have Neriv and Twinflame Tyrant already in play.
  2. Cast Red Dragon. Its own entry ability deals 4 to each opponent.
  3. Because Red Dragon entered this turn, Neriv doubles that damage; Twinflame Tyrant doubles it again.
  4. 4 × 2 × 2 = 16 damage to each opponent, with no attack required.

48 total damage across three opponents

Red Dragon is the source of the damage, not Neriv or Twinflame Tyrant. This is not commander damage.
LINE 04 · EARLY PRESSURE

Anim Pakal + Impact Tremors

Anim Pakal, Thousandth Moon

Attack with Anim Pakal after building her +1/+1 counters. Her attack trigger creates tapped, attacking Gnome tokens equal to her power. Each token entering triggers Impact Tremors for 1 damage to every opponent.

If Anim Pakal has power 3, three Gnomes enter and Tremors deals 3 to each opponent (9 across three opponents).

The tokens enter attacking, but are not declared attackers. Neriv does not double Impact Tremors damage.

Hanweir Garrison + Isshin

Hanweir Garrison

With Isshin, Two Heavens as One in play, declare Hanweir Garrison as an attacker. Isshin makes its attack trigger trigger an additional time, creating four 1/1 Human tokens in total.

Four new attackers from one attack declaration, before other token multipliers.

The tokens enter attacking. They do not trigger their own attack abilities.
LINE 06 · MIDGAME EXPLOSION

Hero of Bladehold + Mondrak

Hero of Bladehold

Attack with Hero of Bladehold while Mondrak, Glory Dominus is in play. The Hero’s token-creation trigger makes four 1/1 Soldiers instead of two.

Four Soldiers enter attacking; Hero’s battle cry trigger also boosts other attacking creatures when it resolves.

Red Dragon + Neriv

Red Dragon

With Neriv already on the battlefield, cast Red Dragon. Its enters-the-battlefield ability deals 4 damage to each opponent, with Red Dragon itself as the source. Neriv doubles that damage because Red Dragon entered this turn.

8 damage to each opponent, 24 across three opponents, without combat.

This is noncombat damage and not commander damage.
LINE 08 · MIDGAME EXPLOSION

Mirkwood Bats + a token burst

Mirkwood Bats

With Mirkwood Bats on the battlefield, make four creature tokens from an attack-trigger engine. Each token creation causes Bats to deal 1 damage to each opponent.

Four token creations yield 4 damage to each opponent (12 across three opponents).

If Bats itself entered this turn and Neriv is on the battlefield, its creature-sourced damage is doubled. Token entry alone does not make existing Bats eligible.
LINE 09 · GAME-ENDING HAYMAKER

Enduring Courage + Neriv (no Tyrant)

Enduring Courage

With Enduring Courage in play, cast Neriv. Courage gives Neriv +2/+0 and haste. Attack an opponent who cannot block its flying attack.

Neriv is 6 power; its own damage-doubling ability makes the unblocked hit 12 commander damage.

This is a powerful baseline that becomes lethal when Twinflame Tyrant is also present, as shown in Line 02.
LINE 10 · GAME-ENDING HAYMAKER

Red Dragon + Twinflame Tyrant (no Neriv)

Twinflame Tyrant

With Twinflame Tyrant on the battlefield, cast Red Dragon. Its entry ability deals 4 to each opponent, doubled by Tyrant.

8 damage to each opponent (24 across three opponents) without needing Neriv.

A useful fallback line when the commander is removed or too expensive to recast.

Turn-by-turn piloting

Turns 1–2 · Build the runway

Prioritize untapped colored mana, a mana rock, and cheap engines such as Ocelot Pride or Impact Tremors when the matchup allows. A fast hand still needs access to white and red.

Turns 3–4 · Generate value

Deploy Anim Pakal, Hanweir Garrison, Professional Face-Breaker or another engine that rewards attacks. Avoid attacking a valuable creature into a poor trade merely to generate one token.

Turns 5–6 · Set the trap

Look for an arrival turn with Neriv, haste support, freshly entered tokens or direct creature damage. Track which creatures actually entered this turn; do not assume every existing creature gets doubled damage.

Turn 7+ · Sound the alarm

Sequence attack triggers, token creation and damage sources carefully. Protect the board with Clever Concealment or Flawless Maneuver where relevant. If combat stalls, lean on Impact Tremors, Mirkwood Bats and Warleader's Call.

Rules corner

What exactly does Neriv double?

Neriv's effect applies to damage dealt by creatures that entered the battlefield this turn. It can include noncombat damage dealt by those creatures, but it does not automatically double damage dealt by an enchantment or artifact.

Does Neriv double Warstorm Surge?

No. Warstorm Surge itself deals the damage, even though a creature's entry caused its trigger.

Do tokens that enter attacking trigger “whenever this creature attacks”?

No. Entering the battlefield attacking is not the same as being declared as an attacker. They can still deal combat damage if they remain attacking and unblocked.

Does Isshin double every token trigger?

No. Isshin adds a trigger only when a creature attacking causes a triggered ability of a permanent you control to trigger. Check the precise trigger condition and source.

Can multiple damage multipliers stack?

Yes, when each applicable replacement effect covers the same damage event. Determine the source, controller and timing, then apply the relevant effects in the proper order.

What beats the deck?

Board wipes

Overextending into sweepers is dangerous. Preserve rebuild tools and don't commit every engine when you already have meaningful pressure.

Mana friction

Three-color sequencing matters. Fetch and shock decisions should support the next two turns, not only the current spell.

Attack restrictions

Pillow-fort effects, repeated removal and large defensive boards can constrain combat payoffs. Noncombat damage helps, but is not a complete substitute.

Engine dependency

Many cards are strongest in combination. Prioritize pieces that function independently when opponents have substantial interaction.
